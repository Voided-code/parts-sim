        How Parts Sim works

        Linear static (bend test). The part is voxelised into 8-node hexahedral elements with incompatible bending modes and solved with a multigrid-preconditioned conjugate-gradient solver on the GPU (WebGPU), or on the CPU when no GPU is available. Stresses are recovered at the element corners and averaged, like the nodal stress plots in commercial FEA.

        Break test. A progressive-damage model: the voxel that first reaches the material's strength is removed, the model is re-solved, and this repeats until the load path is severed. It shows where cracks start and how they run.

        Nonlinear static. Large rotations (co-rotational elements) and metal plasticity (von Mises yield with hardening up to the tensile strength at the elongation at break), solved by Newton iterations with automatic load steps. It can ramp the load until the part collapses or tears, then unloads to show the permanent bend.

        Frequency and buckling. The lowest natural frequencies (with effective mass) and buckling load factors come from a block eigen-solver (LOBPCG) preconditioned by the same multigrid. Without fixtures the part is free-floating and its six rigid-body modes are skipped.

        Fatigue. Stress-life (S-N) estimate from the static stresses: Basquin line from the tensile strength to the fatigue strength, a surface-finish factor for metals and the Goodman mean-stress correction.

        Drop test. Explicit dynamics on the GPU: the part hits a rigid floor at the speed of a free fall from the drop height; the peak stress anywhere during the impact is kept.

        Linear dynamic. Modal superposition with the mode-acceleration correction: frequency sweeps (harmonic loads or base shaking), shock pulses, sine bursts and a synthetic earthquake, with modal damping.

        Optimization. Topology optimization (SIMP, density filter) removes material while keeping the part as stiff as possible for a target mass. The size and material study finds the smallest size of each material that keeps the safety factor, and ranks them by weight or cost.

        Thermal. Steady or transient heat conduction with fixed temperatures, heat inputs and convection, solved with a multigrid-preconditioned conjugate-gradient method.

        Airflow. A 3D lattice-Boltzmann (D3Q19) solver with a Smagorinsky turbulence model runs on the GPU through WebGPU, or on the CPU as a fallback. Curved walls are placed at their true position on the grid (interpolated bounce-back). Pressure drag, lift and surface pressure come from the time-averaged flow. Drag of bluff shapes is typically within 10–30% of wind-tunnel data. Lift on thin wings is under-predicted because the chord only spans a few dozen cells. Use it to compare designs, see the flow and estimate wind loads, not for certification.

        SolidWorks files. Parts and assemblies from SolidWorks 2015 or newer open without SolidWorks, using the display mesh saved in the file. Assemblies need their part files alongside them: the desktop app finds them in the same folder; in a browser, select them together. Older files, or parts saved without display data, need a STEP export.

        Picking. Add a fixture or load, then click faces (or switch to Brush and paint an area). Drag the purple arrow's tail to aim a force, or use the direction buttons.

        Everything runs on your computer. Nothing is uploaded. Results are engineering estimates: check critical parts with a physical test or a certified analysis.
